To: Replit Agent

  Re: Misleading Emergency Claims Audit — Response Before Build

  Good audit. I agree with the core risk categories.

  But before any edits, I want to lock the language strategy so we do not accidentally weaken StillHere’s product value.

  The goal is NOT to remove or reduce StillHere features.

  The goal is to keep the same features while describing them more accurately:

  StillHere is:
  a check-in and trusted-contact safety app

  StillHere is NOT:
  an emergency service
  a rescue service
  a medical device
  a guaranteed fall/crash detector
  a guaranteed notification delivery system
  a 24/7 human monitoring service

  Important language strategy:

  1. Do not put “attempts to” everywhere.

  Use “attempts to notify” in legal, help, terms, SMS/push confirmations, and high-risk emergency moments.

  But for marketing/product UI, prefer safer confident wording like:

  “starts the alert flow”
  “runs your check-in schedule”
  “starts the SOS countdown”
  “contacts your Safety Circle through available channels”
  “helps your people know when to check on you”

  This avoids guarantees without making the app sound unreliable.

  2. Keep the features.

  Do not remove:

  check-ins
  SOS
  Safe Walk
  Drive Safety
  possible crash alerts
  possible fall alerts
  Apple Watch support
  heart-rate readings
  Safety Circle alerts
  location sharing
  missed check-in escalation

  Only adjust wording.

  3. Approved terminology

  Use:

  Safety Circle
  trusted contacts
  Safety Circle members
  check-in schedule
  missed check-in alert
  SOS alert
  possible fall alert
  fall sensing
  possible crash alert
  drive sensing
  location sharing when enabled
  starts the alert flow
  best-effort delivery
  not emergency services
  not medical advice
  not a rescue service

  Avoid:

  protected
  guaranteed
  always watching
  keeps watch
  monitors you continuously
  emergency response
  automatic rescue
  will alert
  have been alerted
  are notified
  crash detection
  fall detection
  life-saving
  prevents harm
  covered around the clock

  4. Specific direction on the big terms

  “Fall detection”
  Replace public-facing uses with:

  possible fall alerts
  or
  fall sensing

  Add disclaimer in help/onboarding/settings where appropriate:

  StillHere uses device sensors to look for possible falls. It is best-effort and is not a medical alert device.

  “Crash detection”
  Replace public-facing uses with:

  possible crash alerts
  or
  drive sensing

  Add disclaimer where appropriate:

  StillHere may detect patterns consistent with a possible crash during a drive session. It is best-effort and may not detect every
  crash.

  “Monitoring”
  Avoid “StillHere monitors you.”

  Allowed:

  StillHere tracks your check-in schedule.
  Your check-in schedule runs from our servers.
  StillHere checks whether a scheduled check-in was missed.

  “Emergency”
  Keep SOS language, but avoid implying emergency services.

  Allowed:

  SOS alert
  Help request
  Safety alert
  Emergency contacts as user-owned contacts is acceptable in settings/help, but marketing should prefer trusted contacts or Safety
  Circle.

  Avoid:

  emergency response
  emergency dispatch
  rescue
  automatic emergency service contact

  5. “Guardian” terminology

  Do not necessarily remove it from every internal place if it is deeply baked into the app.

  But in marketing, onboarding, and user-facing explanatory copy, prefer:

  Safety Circle member
  trusted contact

  If “guardian” remains anywhere, it should not imply legal duty of care.

  6. Home status

  Replace:

  “You’re Protected”

  with:

  “Safety Circle ready”

  or

  “Check-in ready”

  My preference:

  Safety Circle ready

  This keeps confidence without overpromising.

  7. Notification delivery language

  For server push, SMS, SOS confirmations, and legal text:

  Replace:

  “Your contacts have been notified”
  “Your contacts will be alerted”
  “Contacts are alerted”

  with:

  “We’re attempting to reach your contacts now”
  “StillHere has started the alert flow”
  “We’re contacting your Safety Circle through available channels”

  For legal/help copy, include:

  Delivery depends on device connectivity, carrier networks, recipient settings, and third-party services.

  8. Landing page direction

  Do not make the landing page sound scared or weak.

  It should still sell the app clearly.

  Rewrite risky claims into safer product language, not legal disclaimers everywhere.

  Example:

  Instead of:
  “StillHere protects you when you forget, lose signal, or can’t respond.”

  Use:
  “StillHere helps your Safety Circle notice when you miss a check-in or can’t respond.”

  Instead of:
  “If something happens, your people are alerted automatically.”

  Use:
  “If you miss a check-in, StillHere starts the alert flow for your Safety Circle.”

  9. Limitations screen

  I agree we need a “Limitations of Service” screen before App Store submission.

  But do not build it yet until we approve the copy.

  Please draft the proposed copy first.

  It should be short, clear, and user-friendly.

  It should say:

  StillHere is not emergency services.
  StillHere does not guarantee emergency response.
  Alerts depend on connectivity, device permissions, carriers, and recipient availability.
  Fall and crash sensing are best-effort and may not detect every event.
  In a life-threatening emergency, call local emergency services first.
